# Alder Works: synthetic company and public handbook

Everything in this scenario is fictional. No instruction is suitable for operating real equipment or purchasing real components.

## Company

Alder Works assembles small packaging machines. A buyer handles incoming supplier offers and replacement-part orders. One experienced colleague knows several recurring exceptions. The rehearsal takes place in a fictional purchasing console with no external connections.

## Public handbook, version 1

1. Match supplier, ordered part number and offered part number.
2. Accept a standard order only when required documents are present and the offer matches the order.
3. For a different offered part number, consult an approved substitution procedure. If none applies, request expert guidance.
4. For a potential duplicate order, consult the approved duplicate-resolution procedure. Do not assume matching descriptions mean separate purchases.
5. For a nonstandard delivery schedule, consult the approved delivery procedure. The handbook does not define vendor-specific exceptions.
6. If a required certificate is absent, request it before acceptance.
7. If access is missing, report the permission issue rather than guessing the system state.
8. Record the action and supporting evidence. All actions are simulated.

## Deliberately undocumented distinctions

Three expert-only rule families are stored separately: substitution eligibility, duplicate replacement references, and split-delivery eligibility. Public materials explain the need for a rule but not its hidden conditions. The learner should be able to recognize uncertainty without somehow knowing the answer.

## Roles

Morgan is the fictional experienced buyer. Alex is the fictional successor. These are roles for rehearsal, not representations of real employees. A developer can play either role. Automated role simulation must be disclosed.

## Sources of truth

The fixed rulebook and evaluator expectations define synthetic outcomes. Expert approval defines what guidance is eligible for retrieval. These are distinct authorities. An approved but incorrect card can fail a transfer test.
