# Business hypotheses and discovery plan

## Initial market hypothesis

Start with a team that handles frequent, inspectable operational decisions and faces an upcoming handover. Purchasing is the prepared demo domain because task inputs, decisions and exceptions can be represented without physical machinery. It is not yet a validated customer segment.

A suitable initial pilot has one willing expert, one willing successor, a manager who protects their time, accessible historical examples and objective or reviewable completion criteria. Avoid tasks where a mistaken rehearsal could affect customers or equipment.

## Value hypothesis

The customer wants fewer interruptions to unavailable experts and fewer handover failures. The initial deliverable is a bounded handover assessment plus repaired guidance and retest evidence. A later subscription could maintain those tests as work changes. No price, willingness to pay or savings claim is established.

## Discovery questions for a future practitioner

- Tell us about the last time work stopped because a particular colleague was unavailable.
- What could the replacement see, and what could they not figure out?
- How was the problem finally resolved? Which missing fact made the difference?
- How do you currently decide that a handover is complete?
- Which tasks have observable right and wrong outcomes?
- Which information could be used in a safe rehearsal?
- Who would approve this activity, and who would pay for it?
- What would make you decline participation?

Ask for events and artifacts before opinions about the proposed product. Do not treat enthusiasm as evidence of demand.

## Small pilot proposal

Select one narrow workflow. Record a baseline with current materials, capture the missing distinctions, then test fresh cases. Keep expert time, completion time, incorrect actions, escalations and evidence quality. Review surprising failures with the practitioner. No unattended live business actions.

## Reasons to stop or change direction

If gaps are mostly permissions or broken systems, this is not primarily a knowledge-capture problem. If successors already perform well with existing materials, the benefit may be too small. If generic interview notes perform equally well at lower cost, improve the workflow or narrow the claim. If valid cases require months of simulator construction, choose a more tractable domain.

A public demo demonstrates technical execution. A real pilot tests usefulness. Repeated paid adoption would test commercial value. These are separate milestones.
