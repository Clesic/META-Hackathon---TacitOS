# Missing Monday: prove the handover before the expert leaves

## Problem

Experienced employees know exceptions, diagnostic checks, personal handoffs and the reasons behind procedures that never reach a handbook. The team often discovers these dependencies only when that person is unavailable. Recording an interview does not establish that another person can use the information successfully.

## Proposition

Missing Monday rehearses an expert's absence while there is still time to repair the handover. It follows a closed loop:

**Rehearse work → expose a dependency → ask a precise question → approve guidance → test transfer on a fresh case.**

The initial user is an operations lead responsible for a handover. The expert controls what is approved. The successor provides the practical test. The buyer hypothesis is a team facing retirement, rotation, leave or a fragile single-person process.

## Example

A purchaser receives an alternative part number from a familiar supplier. The handbook requires an exact match. The experienced buyer knows that this alternative is acceptable for one machine revision, with a particular certificate. The successor cannot determine whether to accept it.

Missing Monday records that specific blockage and asks the expert which evidence distinguishes an acceptable substitution. The expert explains the condition, reviews the generated guidance and approves it. The successor then handles a different order. Correctly accepting a permitted substitution and rejecting a superficially similar prohibited one provide evidence that the condition transferred.

This example is fictional. It is not manufacturing advice.

## What the product does

1. Scope one workflow and list the available documents, tools and authoritative outcomes.
2. Prepare realistic rehearsal cases, including cases where the handbook is insufficient.
3. Observe only the agreed rehearsal actions and evidence requests.
4. Separate a knowledge gap from missing permissions, a technical failure or an unclear task.
5. Ask the expert a focused question grounded in the actual failed attempt.
6. Produce a small, conditional guidance card linked to the answer and source evidence.
7. Require explicit expert approval of the exact version.
8. Run a fresh transfer case with that guidance available and the expert unavailable.
9. Record the result and any remaining dependency.

The system can propose new challenges, but model-generated cases must pass validation before they count as evidence. The model does not set the official answer and then grade itself.

## The differentiated claim

Existing approaches already cover interviews, searchable expertise, interactive machine teaching, simulations and absence exercises. Missing Monday's product hypothesis is that one inexpensive, repeatable workflow can connect dependency discovery to focused capture and independent transfer verification.

We do not claim to invent knowledge transfer or to prove that no comparable product exists. The potential disruption is economic: teams could run a concrete handover check repeatedly without commissioning a large knowledge-management project. That hypothesis needs field evidence.

## What success means

For a specified workflow and test set, a successor completes more valid tasks with less expert intervention, without increasing incorrect actions. Report the actual number and nature of tested cases. A successful rehearsal does not certify the whole role, all future cases, or the person's absence in a real emergency.

## Why AI is central

The model connects a stalled attempt to the missing distinction, forms a useful question, converts the answer into conditional guidance, and suggests cases that might expose an overgeneralization. The runtime preserves evidence and enforces review and evaluation boundaries. A static checklist alone cannot adapt those questions to new expert answers; a chatbot alone does not demonstrate transfer.

## Longer-term direction

Maintain a test suite for operational knowledge. When a process, supplier or system changes, rerun affected rehearsals and reopen dependencies that no longer pass. Over time, the organization gains a maintained record of what can be handed over, what remains uncertain, and which knowledge needs attention.

Later capabilities could include approved business-system connectors, version-aware retesting and scheduled handover exercises. They are not part of the first demo.
